USB sound, 2026-11-15

Scope
  ReactOS 0.4.16 has the source of a USB Audio Class 1.0 driver
  (drivers/usb/usbaudio) but does not build or ship it, and the driver could
  not have worked: the kernel streaming library it sits on never completed a
  queued sound buffer. compat/reactos/usb-audio.patch makes it work in the
  emulator: playback, recording, volume and mute. The controller driver of
  this project carries the stream (evidence/isochronous-transfers.txt) and
  needed no change.
  Everything below is from the emulator. No real USB sound device has been
  tried, on the board or anywhere else.

Files
  compat/reactos/usb-audio.patch        against the ReactOS 0.4.16 source
  compat/reactos/usbaudio/usbaudio.inf  installs the driver for USB\CLASS_01
  tests/sound/sndtest.c                 plays, records and reads the mixer
                                        through winmm, inside ReactOS
  tests/sound/tonecheck.py              compares QEMU's packet capture with
                                        the tone sndtest plays
  tests/sound/reccheck.py               checks a recording of the test
                                        microphone (tests/isoch/redir_device.py
                                        --microphone)
  The patch produces four modules: usbaudio.sys (new) and replacements for
  ks.sys, wdmaud.sys and wdmaud.drv. GETTING_STARTED.txt says how to build
  and place them. They are not in the installer or in any release.

What was wrong, in the order it was met
  1. No install file. usbaudio.inf binds the driver to the audio function
     usbccgp exposes and pulls in ReactOS's own sound stack (ks.inf,
     wdmaudio.inf).
  2. mmixer (in wdmaud.drv and wdmaud.sys) asserted and then indexed with an
     unset pin number on a filter whose wave path and controls are on one
     filter, as they are for USB audio. controls.c: the pin is its own
     destination when nothing lies behind it. The kernel copy runs only on a
     freshly installed sound stack, where it stopped the machine.
  3. ks.sys freed a pin twice when the driver refused to create it (first
     open with a sample rate the device lacks): bug check 0x19.
  4. ks.sys had only a prototype of the pin queue: frames were never given
     back, leftover DbgBreakPoint calls, no reset, no flush on stop or close,
     KsPinAttemptProcessing unimplemented. pin.c now keeps a count per stream
     irp and completes it when the last stream pointer lets go, runs the
     driver's process routine on the pin's worker under the processing mutex,
     handles IOCTL_KS_RESET_STATE, flushes on stop and close, and frees the
     pin on close.
  5. usbaudio's streaming was written against that prototype. Its pin.c is
     new: requests owned by the pin (8 of 10 packets each), render copies
     the client's frames into them a millisecond packet at a time and lets
     queued sound play out on stop and close; capture keeps them in flight
     and copies packet by packet, by each packet's real length. Close now
     succeeds, returns the interface to its idle setting and frees the pin.
  6. Volume and mute asked for the master channel always (QEMU's card has a
     per-channel volume and refused). filter.c maps the channel the mixer
     names to the unit's own controls. The stream position is reported.
  7. wdmaud.drv answered an unsupported format with a general error, on
     which the wave mapper gives up; it now answers WAVERR_BADFORMAT and the
     mapper converts (44.1 kHz, 22 kHz, mono, 8 bit) to what the device has.

Emulator, QEMU 10.2.1/KVM, qemu-xhci, ReactOS 0.4.16 from the project's stick
image, modules built with i686-w64-mingw32 GCC 13 (Debug)
  Devices: QEMU usb-audio (playback, 48 kHz 16 bit stereo, mute and volume)
  and the test microphone (capture, same format, packets of 47, 48 and 49
  samples). Playback is judged on QEMU's own capture of the USB packets,
  recording on the file the guest wrote.

  Install on an image that never had a sound device: the second boot
    installs both devices without a question, the third plays the logon
    sound and passes the 20 s test below. No assertion, no bug check.
  Playback, device opened directly at 48 kHz: 3 s, 2 s (reopened), 30 s:
    every packet 192 bytes, every sample identical to what was played
    (30 000 packets for the 30 s run), none failed.
  Playback through the wave mapper: 44.1 kHz and 22.05 kHz streams and
    ReactOS_Notify.wav (PlaySound) play, converted to 48 kHz; the logon
    sound plays at every boot.
  Recording: 5 s and 20 s, every sample the microphone sent, in order,
    none missing (960 000 samples in the 20 s run).
  Both at once on the two devices, 20 s: both results as above.
  Mixer: one "Speaker" line with Volume and Mute. Three quarters volume on
    the left channel lowered QEMU's output from 11295 to 9177 on that
    channel only; mute gave silence; both restored.
  Unplugging the card while it plays: the program ends, the device leaves
    the list, the system shuts down normally. Plugged in again it returns
    and plays.
  Without the kernel debugger (normal boot entry): the 30 s run passes.
  AC97 card with the replaced ks.sys, wdmaud.sys and wdmaud.drv: plays, the
    mixer lists its lines as before. (Its recording returns no data in this
    setup with the stock files as well; QEMU has no sound input here.)

Not verified
  - Any real USB sound device. The four modules and usbaudio.inf were copied
    to the board's test stick on 2026-11-15; that stick has not been booted
    since.
  - Timing. QEMU's capture shows the packets but not when a real device
    would have received them; the longest gap between two packets in a run
    was 1.1 to 4.4 ms on the host's clock.
  - One device with playback and recording (a headset): the two directions
    were tested on two devices.
  - Devices with several sample rates, more than two channels, 24 bit
    samples, or high speed with other than one packet a millisecond.
  - Speakers which steer the rate through a feedback endpoint: it is not
    read, so such a device would drift.
  - USB Audio Class 2.0 devices: not supported by this driver at all.

Known gaps left in the driver
  - Removing the device frees nothing (USBAudioPnPRemove is still a stub;
    ReactOS logs "Leaking device"). Survived in the tests above.
  - Only PCM. The descriptors of other formats run into assertions in
    filter.c in a debug build, and a failed assertion stops the machine.
  - A program that opens the device itself with a format it lacks is
    refused; conversion happens only through the wave mapper.
